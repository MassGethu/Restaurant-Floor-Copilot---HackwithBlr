# Restaurant shift memory demonstration

Recording gate: run the real-service evaluator and verify connected browser workflows first. The current implementation must not be narrated as live-verified until that evidence exists. Substitute actual recorded results for every outcome below; keep failures and limitations honest.

Target: 4 minutes, English narration, 1080p or higher, screen capture with optional talking head. Use large fonts and hide account/credential windows. Show the controlled-input disclosure throughout floor footage. All operational episodes below are explicitly synthetic.

## 0:00 to 0:25 — The problem

Show Floor and its real detection feed, then the four-person queue and eligible T2/T3 alternatives. Pause the initial vacant interval after confirming readiness.

Narration: “This application observes table occupancy and checks seating eligibility. But a manager can know something a camera cannot: during a delivery rush, the pickup area beside T2 causes interruptions. The next manager should not have to rediscover that.”

## 0:25 to 1:10 — Teach an operational episode

In Supervisor, confirm the staff-supplied rush context. Record the correction and choose T3. Show pending changing to retained, then inspect a real source ID and recalled memory. Record a separate synthetic outcome report. Do not describe approval as evidence of success.

Narration: “We retain the reason and its conditions. A later outcome is separate evidence, and here it is a labeled scenario report.”

## 1:10 to 2:05 — Show the causal comparison

Start a new logical shift, reconfirm the rush, and ask for advice with the same-input comparison enabled. Show the original allocator choice, the actual model output without memory, and the actual memory-backed output. Expand the supporting memory and explain why its context applies. Approve the validated alternative.

Narration: “The floor, question, model and eligible candidates are identical. Only the memory evidence changes. This is the actual result from our configured services.”

If the expected change does not occur, record that result and fix/evaluate before making a stronger claim. Never substitute a fake response or edit a failure into a success.

## 2:05 to 2:45 — Recovery and handover

Use the prepared dirty-T3 scenario. Ask which existing task prepares the remembered alternative. Approve priority, refresh, then approve role-matching assignment. Show that the table remains unavailable until staff confirms cleaning. Generate handover and distinguish its cited history from the current task list.

Narration: “The same experience informs recovery and handover. The agent proposes; staff approves. Assignment never means clean.”

## 2:45 to 3:25 — Persistence and revision

Show the application restart and actual recall from its persistent Hindsight bank, or the unedited evidence captured by the evaluation script if startup would interrupt the recording. Clearly label any recorded segment. Report that pickup moved, supersede the lesson, and show the changed evidence and actual revised advice. Explain that old history remains visible.

## 3:25 to 4:00 — Architecture and evidence

Show the architecture diagram and real evaluation report: actual trial count, consistency, quiet-period control, and failures. Explain that fixtures are synthetic, observed outcomes are staff reports, and no real restaurant business improvements have been measured. Close on the promise that the next shift can benefit from the last shift's experience.

The first 60 seconds should already reveal the problem, retained reason, and later retrieval. Full evidence and controls follow.
